# Restricted Autonomy–Stimulation Conditioning (RASC)

**Restricted Autonomy, Stimulation Seeking, and Threat-Linked Relief: A Developmental Conditioning Hypothesis for Context-Dependent Attention Difficulties**

## Abstract

The RASC hypothesis proposes that repeated restriction of age-appropriate childhood choice, exploration, and peer interaction may shape context-specific patterns of stimulation seeking and restlessness. Unauthorized self-directed exploration may bring rapid relief followed by fear of punishment. Repeated relief-then-fear learning could modify attention-related behavior and potentially amplify existing ADHD symptoms in some individuals. This conceptual paper proposes operational measures, falsifiable predictions, rival explanations, a prospective cohort, a factorial experiment, and an intervention study.

## Research status

**Unpublished, untested hypothesis.** No participants were recruited, no dataset was analyzed, and no original experiments were conducted. RASC is not an established cause of ADHD or a validated treatment. ADHD has substantial genetic and neurodevelopmental influences, and the proposal explicitly considers reverse causation and other confounders.
