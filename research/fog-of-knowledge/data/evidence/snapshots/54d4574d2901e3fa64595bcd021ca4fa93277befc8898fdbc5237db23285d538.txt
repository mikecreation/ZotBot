paradox
A
paradox
is an assertion that is apparently self-contradictory, though based on a valid
deduction
from acceptable
premises
.
Paradoxes typically lead to a reevaluation of the axioms of mathematics.  after axioms are assumed so that the paradox is averted, the statement is still usually referred to as a paradox.
Occasionally, one may refer to a surprising result as a paradox. Such is the case in the
birthday paradox
, which is not apparently self-contradictory.
Examples of paradoxes include:
•
Banach-Tarski paradox
•
binary tree paradox
•
birthday paradox (
http://planetmath.org/BirthdayParadox
)
•
Burali-Forti paradox
•
Cantor’s paradox
•
Hausdorff paradox
•
Russell’s paradox
•
Simpson’s paradox
•
Zeno’s paradox
