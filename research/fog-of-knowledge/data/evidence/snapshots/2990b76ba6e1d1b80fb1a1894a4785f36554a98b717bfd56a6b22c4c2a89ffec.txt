measurable space
A
measurable space
is a set
E
together with a
collection
ℬ
of subsets of
E
which is a
sigma algebra
.
The elements of
ℬ
are called
measurable sets
.
A measurable space is the correct object on which to define a
measure
;
ℬ
will be the collection of sets which actually have a measure. We normally want to ensure that
ℬ
contains all the sets we will ever want to use. We usually cannot take
ℬ
to be the collection of all subsets of
E
because the
axiom of choice
often allows one to construct sets that would lead to a
contradiction
if we gave them a measure (even zero). For the
real numbers
, Vitali’s
theorem
states that
ℬ
cannot be the collection of all subsets if we hope to have a measure that returns the length of an
open interval
.
