PlanetMath
PlanetMath
is a free, collaborative, online mathematics encyclopedia. The emphasis is on peer review, rigor, openness, pedagogy, real-time content, interlinked content, and community. Intended to be comprehensive, the project is located at the Digital Library Research Lab at Virginia Tech.
PlanetMath was started when the popular free online mathematics encyclopedia
MathWorld
was taken offline by a court injunction as a result of the CRC Press lawsuit against the Wolfram Research company and its employee (and MathWorld’s author)
Eric Weisstein
.
PlanetMath uses the same copyleft as
Wikipedia
: the
http://www.gnu.org/copyleft/fdl.html
GNU Free Documentation License. An author who starts a new article becomes the owner of that article; he or she may then choose to grant editing rights to other
individuals
or groups. All textual content and mathematical
formulas
are written in LaTeX, a typesetting system that requires some learning but is popular among mathematicians because of its
support
of the technical needs of mathematical typesetting and its high-quality output. The user can explicitly create links to other articles, and the system also automatically turns certain words into links to the defining articles. For more details on the automatic linking, see the collaboration on
http://planetmath.org/?op=getobj&from=collab&id=32
PlanetMath automatic reference linking. For more details on controlling the linking of an article, see the collaboration on
http://planetmath.org/?op=getobj&from=collab&id=33
controlling linking The topic area of every article is classified by the
http://planetmath.org/?op=mscbrowse
Mathematics Subject Classification
of the
American Mathematical Society
. Users may attach addenda, errata, and discussions to articles.
The most common method of public communication within PlanetMath is posts. Users can add posts in the
http://planetmath.org/?op=forums
forums as well as attach posts to articles,
http://planetmath.org/?op=globalcors
corrections,
http://planetmath.org/?op=collab
collaborations,
http://planetmath.org/?op=reqlist
requests for new articles, and other posts. A system for private messaging among users is also in place.

Most of the very most basic topics are covered, though PlanetMath is striving to improve coverage of
elementary
and intermediate topics. Due to the increasing popularity of the package PSTricks, more members of PlanetMath are able to incorporate graphics into their articles. This has enabled PlanetMath to cover many elementary and intermediate topics in
geometry
that were once lacking. There are several methods of creating graphics on PlanetMath. For more details on creating graphics on PlanetMath, see the collaboration on
http://planetmath.org/?op=getobj&from=collab&id=53
graphics and PlanetMath.
PlanetMath also has entries on highly advanced and specialized topics. PlanetMath has entries on the integers 42 (
http://planetmath.org/FortyTwo
) and 666 (
http://planetmath.org/NumberOfTheBeast
). The following top-level Mathematics Subject Classification categories have only one or two topic entries at PlanetMath:
•
74-XX, mechanics of deformable solids;
•
76-XX, fluid mechanics;
•
85-XX, astronomy and astrophysics.
