Collatz problem
Collatz problem
(classic problem)
Definition:
Consider the function: if N is odd, 3 × N + 1; else N/2. Does beginning with any positive integer and repeatedly applying the function always yields 1?
Note:
First posed by the German mathematician Lothar Collatz in 1937. Began appearing in print in the 1950's.
The sequence of integers generated are called "hailstones", since they may rise and fall but (presumably) always fall to the ground (the integer 1). Also called the Syracuse problem and many other names.
