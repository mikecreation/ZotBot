Miller-Rabin
Miller-Rabin
(algorithm)
Definition:
A
heuristic
test for prime numbers.  It repeatedly checks if the number being tested, n, is pseudoprime to a randomly chosen base, a, and there are only trivial square roots of 1, modulo n.  In other words, n is surely composite if a
n-1
≠ 1 (mod n), where 0 < a < n.  Some composites may be incorrectly judged to be prime.
Note:
For k repetitions, the chance of incorrectly judging an odd integer greater than 2 to be prime is 2
-k
.  For randomly chosen large integers, a small number of repetitions, say 3, is enough. After
[CLR90, pages 838-844]
.
