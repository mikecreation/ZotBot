# Theory Notes

## Systemic Continuity Theory of Consciousness (SCTC)

SCTC is definition-first. It assigns minimal system-level consciousness to the viable, integrated biological individual itself and separates the faculties that individual can instantiate.

### Primitive target

`C_B(t) := L_B(t)`

`L_B(t)=1` means the same biological individual exists at time `t` as a viable integrated system.

### Faculty exclusion

If a declared faculty can be absent while `L_B=1`, that faculty is not constitutive of system-level consciousness under SCTC.

### Explanatory separation

Failure to explain a nonconstitutive faculty does not by itself amount to failure to define or explain the system-level target. The most important case is phenomenal experience `E`.

### Target substitution

A critic who asks why subjective experience occurs has posed a legitimate question about `E`. It becomes an objection to SCTC only after an independent argument establishes that `C` and `E` must be identical.

### Biological individuality

SCTC inherits the best operational account of biological individuality and explicitly inherits genuine indeterminacy where biology cannot yet draw a determinate boundary.

### Terminal discontinuity

Terminal discontinuity is intervention-relative. A successful rescue path is evidence that the terminal boundary had not yet been crossed under the declared admissible intervention family.

### Rivals

- `C := E` (experience-centered)
- `C := T` (capacity-transition theories such as UAL)
- `C := L_B` (SCTC)

The theory asks that these target assignments be compared openly rather than mixed inside the same noun.
